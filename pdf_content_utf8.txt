Title of the Final Year Project / Dissertation / Thesis:   
A Web Application for Educational Content Sharing, Remote Tutoring, and Gamified 
Learning Support  
Abstract:  
This investigation report crafts and creates an integrated web -based solution, that seeks to 
coag ulate the gap in academic assistance among students of different educational levels. The 
system integrates tutor matching, online delivery of digital content and gamification learning 
features to increase the quality of engagement with student, increase ac cess to educational 
resources and learning across geographical and institutional boundaries. The research paper 
points out the major problems of the current e -learning platforms such as low cross -level 
connectivity, no implementation of motivation factor, or its scalability. The project provides 
the literature review and the comparison of other systems like Moodle, Duolingo, Khan 
Academy, and Coursera to point out the significance of personalization, user motivation, and 
flexibility of delivery method of mo dern educational technology.  
The platform is designed on a React.js frontend, a PostgreSQL database (hosted by Neon) and 
JavaScript -based backend services, therefore, ensuring scalability and simple interaction. To 
make the gifting more motivational and en gagement promoting, gamification elements are 
added which include points, levels and achievements badges. The tutor matching system 
functions to match the learners according to the subject needs and levels of the respective skills 
to allow effective learni ng between peers, as well as exchange of knowledge. Also, content 
sharing features serve various learning material needs, varying between lecture notes and 
interactive modules, therefore, offering inclusivity and flexibility in the learning process.

This investigation report crafts and creates an integrated web -based solution, that seeks to 
coagulate the gap in academic assistance among students of different educational levels. The 
system integrates tutor matching, online delivery of digital content  and gamification learning 
features to increase the quality of engagement with student, increase access to educational 
resources and learning across geographical and institutional boundaries. The research paper points 
out the major problems of the current e-learning platforms such as low cross -level connectivity, 
no implementation of motivation factor, or its scalability. The project provides the literature review 
and the comparison of other systems like Moodle, Duolingo, Khan Academy, and Coursera to 
point  out the significance of personalization, user motivation, and flexibility of delivery method 
of modern educational technology.  
 
The platform is designed on a React.js frontend, a PostgreSQL database (hosted by Neon) and 
JavaScript -based backend services, therefore, ensuring scalability and simple interaction. To make 
the gifting more motivational and engagement promoting, gamification elements are added which 
include points, levels and achievements badges. The tutor matching system functions to match the 
learners according to the subject needs and levels of the respective skills to allow effective learning 
between peers, as well as exchange of knowledge. Also, content sharing features serve various 
learning material needs, varying between lecture notes and interactive modules, therefore, offering 
inclusivity and flexibility in the learning process.  
 
This research result demonstrates that further combining the effects of gamification with the peer -
to-peer learning and content -sharing options is apt to lead to  a substantial increment in engagement 
levels, academic success, and cooperation alliances across the education field.

1.1. Introduction  
The education technology has become innovative in the way students access learning materials 
and communicate with o thers and instructors. Web -based modern platforms currently provide the 
opportunity to organize synchronous (real -time) and asynchronous (self -paced) learning, which 
makes the participation of students located in other geographic regions possible. The plat forms are 
becoming more of a design that is learner -centered at all levels of education, primary to tertiary, 
which is flexible and accessible at a large scale (OECD, 2023).  
 
It has found a marked surge in its application through gamification or the process of levying non -
game situations with the mechanics of games like points, levels, and badges able to motivate the 
learners accordingly (Sailer & Homner, 2020). According to research, gamification can increase 
involvement in any educational environmen t, though its application on various levels of education 
is usually inconsistent. This project serves to fill in that gap by creating a scalable platform 
encompassing all those three useful dimensions (tutor matching, content sharing, gamified 
interactions ) with all the academic levels of the students . 
 
In addition to the provision of academic access, the platform encourages cross -level collaboration 
with the possibility of university students being peer tutors to students and resulting in both 
teaching and  learning processes being valuable. This form of integration promotes lifelong 
inclusive education, which is shared by the national and international education strategies (OECD, 
2025). Speaking of the issues of motivation, the accessibility, and scale, the  platform strives to 
create a more connected and equal digital learning world.

1.2. Problem Background  
 
Problem Statement 1: Inflexible Cross -Level Adaptability  
Existing e -learning systems such as Moodle are largely used at a higher education level yet are 
frequently too complicated or resource -communal to be of use on a secondary or younger grade 
level leading to ineffective use within school settings (OECD, 202 3). On the other hand, Google 
Classroom itself can serve as an effective tool in schools but not so powerful regarding self -
learning and collaboration, which a student needs at the university. This disparity constrains how 
well a single platform can reach all the levels of education.  
 
Problem Statement 2: Size of Tutoring Models  
Face -to-face tutor ing is one of the most effective traditional types of tutoring aimed at personal 
learning but it is too demanding in relation to resources and is hard to scale in cases of high student 
numbers or the absence of sufficient qualified tutors (Kraft & Falken, 2021) . In absence of scalable 
digital solutions, underserved neighborhoods and institutions, are poorly poised to satisfy the 
demand of academic support.  
 
Problem Statement 3: Disjointed Gamification Practice  
The gamification apps (Kahoot! or Duolingo) exh ibit a possible potential to enhance the 
engagement of students but are most commonly applied separately, without the full utilization of 
learning management system (Sailer & Homner, 2020).  Such piecemeal application of game 
mechanics may help to create im mediate enthusiasm, but fail to have lasting results in 
environments that require long -term academic development.

Problem Statement 4: Bandwidth limitations and Accessibility Problems  
Most current online learning systems are based on an intensive multim edia component that does 
not perform as well in scenarios of low bandwidth or mobile first, as it is possible to encounter in 
rural or underserved regions (Sign├⌐, 2023) . It causes the digital divide, the students who have low 
connectivity cannot enjoy all the experiences of studying online.  
 
Problem Statement 5: Low Opportunities to Cross -Level Collaborate  
Existing learning tools do not provide much facilitation of collaborative learning interactions 
between various levels of education - like college students and secondary school students. Such 
unstructured cross -level peer tutoring minimizes access to role m odeling, skill carry -over and 
learning contributions in an educational setting. To create one example, research into the adult 
students of the cross -age peer tutoring in STEAM projects has revealed the effectiveness of older 
students helping younger learne rs acquire the skill of programming and collaboration making, 
proving to be a source of social support and developing pedagogical skills of both of the parties 
(Tenhovirta et al. 2022). Integrating similar structures in the e -learning systems will improve the 
inclusivity and learner to learner related learning within the levels of learners.

3.1.1. Introduction  
The choice of a pro per system development methodology is very essential in ensuring software 
undertakings are well -organized, requirements driven and within the given time -frames and 
budgetary limits. A methodology entails the systematic arrangement of important operations s uch 
as planning, designing, implementation, testing, deployment and reviewing and hence there is 
coordination and clarity with the project lifecycle. Traditional linear approaches may limit the 
flexibility and agility in situations where needs of the users  change or adjustment to features is

Agile approaches have presented a new kind of alternative that focuses on iterative shipment, 
cooperation with stakeholders and flexibility. Instead of traditional sequential models, the Agile 
framework will  split the development process into minimal and repeated iterations through which 
it will be possible to achieve early feedback and flexible transformation (Weichbroth, 2022). This 
would make it especially amenable to the creation of gamified learning envi ronments that require 
maximum usability, user engagement and ongoing enhancement.  
 
Scrum is an Agile -based approach that represents more of a framework of development that breaks 
work into sprints or concentrated work time and includes a high level of feed back by the 
stakeholders and the ability by the team to review the work done and the process (Jain, Jotwani, & 
Agrawal, 2024). This is because the Agile -Scrum method will divide work into parts so that it is 
done in small incremental tasks and that progres s is verified on a regular basis thus enabling each 
functional part of the system, like content sharing tools, gamification features, or tutor matching 
features, to be tailored dynamically to the real feedback of users.

the basis of changing user needs (Digital.ai, 2022).  
 
In this project, being the creation of a gamified educational platform that will include the

one of the most popular Agile frameworks. It divides the work  into identified roles (Scrum Master, 
Product Owner, Development Team), meetings (sprint planning, daily stand -ups, sprint reviews, 
and retrospectives) and even things (product backlog, sprint backlog, and increment). The above 
practices preserve disciplin e, transparency, and accountability and yet they still have a level of 
flexibility and innovation. The given structure would also be appropriate in the academic setting 
in relation to which the documentation of the project, the deliverables tracking, and t he progress 
measurability are also important (Scrum.org, 2023).

learning patterns among some respondents.

gamification is rather worthwhile, there is s till a significant number of users who value deeper, 
more accurate, and applicable educational content. The given findings indicate that an optimal 
balance between the promotion of interactive and motivational features, usability, and the quality 
of learni ng materials should be sought as an improvement strategy. A set of these elements, 
combined together, may solve the problem of the diversified needs of learners and better the overall 
satisfaction with gamified learning platforms.

4.5 Test Case Preparation  
4.5.1 Introduction  
To ascertain that the EduBridge system i s reliable and usable, a number of software testing 
techniques will be employed to address technical correctness as well as real user experience. To 
ensure that bugs are revealed earlier and eliminated more quickly, first will be Unit Testing used 
to valid ate separate functions like login/register, role -based access (student/tutor/admin), course 
creation, lesson viewing, booking requests and progress tracking. Integration Testing will then be 
undertaken to ensure that modules that are interconnected are fun ctioning correctly such as a tutor 
creating a course component and students submitting assignments, booking requests connecting 

CHAI CHARNG SHIN ................................ ................................ ............................  APU3F2505SE
................................ ................................ ................................ ................................ ...........  TP065365  
pg. 219 
ASIA PACIFIC UNIVERSITY OF TECHNOLOGY AND INNOTATION  with tutor availability slot and tutor availability notifications/audit logs. System Testing will then 
be carried out to test th e entire EduBridge workflow on an end-to-end basis to ensure that the entire 
platform is acting as it should in real utilization conditions with students, tutors and administrators. 
Then, User Acceptance Testing (UAT) will be conducted with the representat ive users (students, 
tutors, and admins) to ensure that the system is functional and can be used without any difficulties 
in the real scenarios, e.g., when learning a lesson, completing a piece of work, grading it and 
managing the system operations. Finall y, the Security and Validation Testing will be added to 
ensure the safe usage of the user data and permissions such that unauthorized users are unable to 
use the restricted features of the application and safe input (forms, uploads, and booking details) 
is done. The validity of the prepared test cases by combining these types of testing will assist in 
ensuring that EduBridge is technically stable and meets the expectations of the users in actual 
learning and teaching settings.

ASIA PACIFIC UNIVERSITY OF TECHNOLOGY AND INNOTATION  4.7 Challenging Functions (Sample Code)  
4.7.1 Enrollment Transactions Atomic Course.  
The course enrollment transaction is one of the most dif ficult aspects of EduBridge, which is 
undertaken with course.controller.js of server/src/controllers/course under enrollInCourse function. 
This capability registers a student into a published course and instantly initializes the learning 
tracking informati on by creating an enrollment record and issuing course progress records on all 
the lessons in the course. It employs prisma.transaction in order to make the entire process a single 
atomic operation. In case of failure in any of the steps (e.g., creation of  a progress or updating a 
course counter), any modifications are reversed to make sure that the student is not left in an invalid 
state (e.g. being under enrolment without a progress record).  
 
This is not easy to implement due to the fact that it has to en sure data integrity, concurrency (like 
double - click enroll) and still be performant in cases where a course contains numerous lessons. 
The operation to be performed in the transaction has several dependent operations: course 
validation and pre -existing en rollment, formation of enrollment row, formation of many progress 
rows, and update of course enrolled number. It was tested by failure and rollback behaviors were 
checked to ensure that rollback was correct, a course of 50+ lessons was also tested to check  the 
performance and concurrent enroll requests were checked to make sure that duplicates were not a 
problem.

the badge definitions and badges that the user has already earned, then makes parallel database 
calls to get the user the nec essary statistics and, lastly, executes various badge rules with a switch -

4.7.3  P2P Booking Process.  
 
The third implementation that has been tricky to implement is the tutoring booking, which is added 
in server/src/controllers/booking.controller.js, under createBo okingRequest and acceptBooking. 
This capability is in charge of the entire lifecycle of a tutoring request, beginning with the arrival 
of a booking request by a student, through acceptance of a booking request by a tutor and the 
subsequent dispatch of an e mail notification. The accept role is made up of tight security checks 
where only the designated tutor can authorize the booking and provide rules on state validation 
where only the request with the state PENDING can be accepted.

6.1 Critical Evaluation  
 
EduBridge was able to successfully achieve the main purpose of the project, which is a role -based 
learning platform where students can access lessons, download learning materials and keep track 
of the completion, tutors can create and  manage courses/lessons, and admins can control users and 
approvals. Based on the results in Chapter 5, most of the core workflows are executed correctly 
with high pass rate, indicating that the system is stable for normal user usage. This result confirms 
the current need for better digital learning ecosystems that are capable of delivering content, 
tracking learning progress and managing users effectively (OECD, 2023).  
 
EduBridge also incorporated some basic engagement support in the form of gamification i deas 
(such as lesson completion rewards). Studies from recent years reported the positive effect of 
gamification on improving motivation and learning performance if the gamification approach was 
designed in a meaningful way with its connection to learning outcomes (Bai et al., 2020; 
Lampropoulos et al., 2024). Therefore, EduBridge is not only a functional delivery system for 
learning, but a learning environment that can help users to continue to learn.  
 
6.2 Limitation  
 
One of the limitations of EduBridge wo uld be that learning performance still requires stable 
internet access and appropriate devices, which may limit the usability for students in low access 
environments. This is a problem faced by many online learning systems because not everyone has 
equal ac cess and different digital readiness levels which can impact on learning participation and 
effectiveness (OECD, 2023).

ASIA PACIFIC UNIVERSITY OF TECHNOLOGY AND INNOTATION  Another limitation is in the area of privacy and security, since EduBridge stores user accounts, 
learning records and activity actions. Education platforms need to carefully protect the information 
of learners and ensure the secure design of systems, particularly in areas such as login and access 
control and data handling (UNESCO, 2022). Since the web applications often pose risks like 
broken access control and authentication issues, the EduBridge must continuously enhance 
security checks and enhance the security protection practices based on the known web security 
risks (OWASP, 2021).  
 
6.3 Recommendation  
 
For future improvement, EduBridge should increase learning experience and governance. First, 
the system should enhance the student clarity by including better progress indicators such as course 
completion percentage, lesson completion summary, and achievement tracking. Second, the course 
approval workflow should be made more stringent to ensure that courses are not published until 
after they have been reviewed by an admin after being submitted by the tutor, to prevent 
unreviewed content from being published. These improvements can lead to h igher levels of trust, 
the quality of contents and user incapacity (OECD, 2023).  
 
EduBridge should also improve security and privacy practices such as implementing more precise 
consent notice requirements, improved role -based permissions, activity logging for admin activity, 
and regular security reviews. These are common web risks steps that are carried to support data 
protection learner expectations in digital education systems (OWASP, 2021; UNESCO, 2022). 
With these improvements, EduBridge will be more re liable and safer to use in real usage, and more 
appropriate for future deployment and scaling.
